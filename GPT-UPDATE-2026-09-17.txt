 2. THE BIG FIND: YOUR "CONTRADICTION" WAS ONE PARSER GAP
===============================================================

You flagged that transcript.txt said BOTH

    tier: unverified
    coverage: not checked

and, immediately below,

    transcript_tier: verified
    coverage: pass

You were right that this was incoherent, and right about roughly
where it came from. The actual cause:

  the transcriber writes timestamps as   0:07  words
  the app could read Whisper JSON and SRT, and nothing else

So the app saw a transcript with ZERO timestamped segments, filed
it "unverified", and never ran its own coverage gate. Both lines
were honest; they were describing different layers.

IT HAD A THIRD CONSEQUENCE NOBODY HAD NOTICED. The app deletes a
walk's audio only when coverage PASSES. Coverage had never passed,
on any walk, ever - so no audio had ever been deleted. The owner's
"golden rule" had never once fired.

Fixed by teaching the app the format its own toolchain produces.
Checked against the real walk of 14 Sep: 105 segments, 785s,
coverage passes. The owner re-imported it and it now reads
VERIFIED / COVERAGE PASSED on the phone.

WHAT THIS MEANS FOR YOU: from the next walk onward, a transcript
in a package carries one verification state, not two, and you can
trust the tier line. An "unverified" transcript now genuinely
means a pasted or typed one.

ONE RECOMMENDATION OF YOURS WAS DECLINED, deliberately. You
suggested the transcriber emit structured metadata and the app
read its verification state from that. That would make the app
trust the transcriber's verdict. The app has its own coverage gate
and its own duration; it now checks the timestamps itself. Teach
it the format, do not import the conclusion.

===============================================================
 4. APPROVAL CHANGED. THIS ONE AFFECTS HOW YOU WRITE FILES
===============================================================

The old rule was "every proposed change is approved ONE AT A TIME,
there is no accept-all". The owner reversed it on 16 Sep. His
reasoning:

    the substantive review now happens in conversation, before
    the update file is generated. By the time the table renders,
    the argument is over.

The app now has Select all / Clear. What has NOT changed, and is
not negotiable:

  - every change is listed with current value, proposed value
    and its own reason
  - any single row can be rejected on its own
  - rows arrive UNSELECTED

SO THE WAY YOU WRITE A FILE MATTERS MORE, NOT LESS. He will now
often select all and untick the few he disagrees with. A row he
cannot reject half of is a row that forces him to reject the good
half with the bad. One change, one field, one reason. Never bundle
two ideas.

And the workflow you proposed is the one being used: discuss in
chat first, agree, THEN generate the file. Do not produce an
update file until the conversation has settled.

DUPLICATE WATERING (your section 6) - fixed, and the cause was not
what it looked like. Care events land PENDING and move no derived

 6. WHERE YOUR SECTION 3 WAS WRONG, AND WHAT IS REALLY WRONG
===============================================================

This matters, because you framed a fixed bug as a design
limitation and missed the real problem underneath it.

THE OFFSET RESETS (300s, then 0, 30, 57) WERE A BUG, AND IT WAS
ALREADY FIXED BEFORE YOU SAW THE PACKAGE. The walk clock was being
driven to zero by a timing fault, and the clock is what stamps
marker offsets. The walk's own trail file recorded it happening.
The package you reviewed was recorded on the build that still had
it.

THE REAL PROBLEM IS DIFFERENT AND IS NOT YET FIXED:

    the walk clock says   357 seconds
    the audio says        785 seconds

iOS keeps recording while the app is in the background. The clock
does not. So roughly 428 seconds of that walk are audio with no
clock time behind them, and every marker after a backgrounding is
progressively EARLY against the stitched transcript - not reset,
just drifting behind.

Your rule stands and is correct, for this reason rather than the
one you gave: NEVER TREAT RAW MARKER OFFSETS AS ONE MONOTONIC
TIMELINE ACROSS AN INTERRUPTED WALK.

Being built next, in the conservative form you argued for: the
part boundaries are derived from information the app already
stores, accurate to about three seconds, with NO change to the
recording, saving, stitching or transcription chain. The version
that writes new metadata during capture is held back until the
cheap version is proved inadequate on a real walk. That was the
owner's call and it is the right one.

UNTIL THEN: the words are primary evidence. Markers are weaker
attribution evidence. Where they disagree, the words win.
