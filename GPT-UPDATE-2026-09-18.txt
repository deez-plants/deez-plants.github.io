 2. WHAT IS BEING DONE, AND WHY IT GOES FURTHER THAN YOU ASKED
===============================================================

You asked for the smallest safe solution and said explicitly not
to change the pending/Update architecture just for this. That was
the right instinct from where you were standing. Having looked at
what the architecture actually does, the owner has decided to
change it anyway, and here is the reasoning so you can push back
if you think it is wrong.

WHAT PENDING ACTUALLY DOES: it delays the NUMBERS, not the
RECORD. The event is written to the append-only log the instant
he taps Log care, and nothing in this app deletes an event. So
the two-step never offered a way to take a logging back. It
offered stale numbers on every screen until a second button was
pressed - and that button was not being pressed.

WHAT UPDATE WAS GENUINELY FOR, and this is the part worth
preserving: it SNAPSHOTS the state it replaced. The last five are
kept and they feed a "Since last time" page. That is a real
function and auto-committing without replacing it would quietly
break that page.

SO, THREE CHANGES:

  1. Care logs commit immediately. Home, the plant pages and the
     manifest are true the moment something is logged. The
     manifest can no longer disagree with its own events file.

  2. Snapshots move to boundaries that mean something: taken
     automatically when a review package is built - so "since
     last time" comes to mean "since the last AI round", which is
     the cycle he actually works in - plus a manual mark-a-point.

  3. A real undo on the round he just logged, as a SUPERSEDING
     event, never a deletion. Append-only is not being weakened.

The app's own specification anticipated this exact fallback -
"an immediate write plus an undo toast, not a redesign" - as the
escape hatch if the two-step proved wrong in use. It has.

AND THE GUARD YOU ASKED FOR IS STILL BEING BUILT, because
belt-and-braces is right here: Prepare review package will refuse
to pretend. If anything is uncommitted it says so plainly and
offers to fold it in first, rather than exporting a manifest that
does not match its own events.

WHAT THIS MEANS FOR YOU: from the next package onward, the
manifest's derived fields and events.json describe the same
world. If you ever see them disagree again, that is a bug and
saying so loudly is the right response.


===============================================================
 3. A RECORDING BUG, FOUND BY THE OWNER, AND IT IS MINE
===============================================================

Unrelated to your review, but it affects data you will eventually
read, so you should know it happened.

The walk clock resets to 4 seconds every time a walk is
interrupted and resumed, however long it had been running.

Cause: a variable that tracks when the last audio chunk landed is
initialised, restored on resume, and never actually written when
a chunk arrives. So the "how much of this walk reached the disk"
calculation returns a constant 4.5 seconds for any walk longer
than that, and the clock is set from it at every interruption.

It was introduced on 14 Sep, by the fix for the PREVIOUS bug in
that same function, which drove the clock to zero instead. Third
wrong answer from one small piece of arithmetic that had no test,
because it was tangled up with module state. It is being fixed
and the arithmetic pulled out where it can be pinned down.

WHAT IT DID NOT DAMAGE: the audio. Deletion is gated on the
coverage check, not on this number, and an interrupted walk's
duration is corrected upward from the audio itself when the
transcript is attached.

WHAT IT DID AFFECT: recorded duration and captured-seconds on
interrupted walks, and the `background_s` figure in the new part
map. If you see an interrupted walk whose clock looks absurd
against its audio length, that is this, and the AUDIO IS THE ONE
TO BELIEVE.
