===============================================================
 2. CONTRACT CORRECTION — THE REASON IS NOW KEPT
===============================================================

THE PROBLEM. When a row was applied, the resulting entry
recorded the new value, that it came from the AI, and which
package — AND DISCARDED THE REASON. A year later a change read

  "interval 10 → 7, by AI, from PKG-2026-09-17-1"

and why was gone, recoverable only from an update file nobody
keeps.

WHY THIS WAS A CONTRACT VIOLATION, not merely a gap. The
installed Project Instructions require "Preserve provenance and
append-only history". The whole argument for never deleting
anything is that a change made in 2026 and the rating that
followed it read identically in 2031. The number survived; the
reasoning evaporated.

FIXED. The reason is now written to the entry's note field. It
travels in every future package, appears in history, and
survives a backup and restore like any other entry content.

AN OVER-LONG REASON IS NOW REFUSED, not truncated, at 400
characters — the documented cap for a note. Half a reason is
worse than a rejected file, because nobody can tell it is half.

**CONSEQUENCE FOR REVIEW WRITING: a reason is now permanent
record rather than transient justification.** It is worth
writing as something a stranger will read in five years. This
strengthens, rather than changes, the existing rule against
boilerplate: a repeated sentence was already inadequate
reasoning, and is now inadequate reasoning written into the
record 21 times.

NOT RETROSPECTIVE. Changes applied before today have no stored
reason and cannot acquire one. The 228 changes of the first real
review keep their values and their provenance, but their
reasoning survives only in the update file and the conversation.


===============================================================
 3. THE SIX REFERENCE FIELDS ARE NOW OWNER-EDITABLE
===============================================================

THE PROBLEM. environment, repotting, pruning, pests, season and
propagation are documented `editable_by: both`, and the app
provided a way for only one of the two. They rendered read-only
with "Ask the AI for this in your next review."

So THE AI WAS THEIR SOLE WRITER. A value the owner disagreed
with required a package, a conversation and an import to
correct — to fix a typo. Every other field on every other screen
they own outright.

FIXED. Each is editable in place on "More about this plant",
with the same 200-character limit the importer now enforces, so
the owner's editor and a proposal are held to one standard.

WHY THIS MATTERS TO REVIEW BEHAVIOUR, and it does:

  An owner edit is an ordinary Edit entry. So if they correct one
  of these fields AFTER a package is generated, the conflict rule
  fires on the next update naming that field — the row is shown
  with their value, their date and the AI's, and they choose.

  **PREVIOUSLY THAT COULD NOT HAPPEN**, because they could not
  edit these fields at all. A proposal now has a real chance of
  meeting a human correction, and should be written expecting
  that rather than assuming a blank slate.

CLASSIFICATION: a defect against the documented field
permissions, not new capability. Flagged to the owner as a
judgement call before building; they approved it explicitly.
